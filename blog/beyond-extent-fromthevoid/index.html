Beyond Extent From the Void Team Challenge

Recently I took part in Beyond Extents Team Challenge, Beyond the Void, check out what we made!

Hello there, I’ve been hard at work on a 3D challenge these past 5 weeks which I’m excited to share has wrapped up! This challenge took place on the Beyond Extent discord where we were given a theme and 5 weeks to tackle making a full-scale game ready environment as a team of 5!

What was the theme?

The theme for this challenge is From the Void, and here is what we came up with!

Music Credit: Run Rabbit Productions - The Exorcist https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kBhXebL6mx8

This environment takes the theme in a more narrative route, showing the life of an elderly Victorian pianist who has fallen into darker habits warping her self image. The void representing a metaphorical loss of the sense of self when looking into her grand mirror. The attic represents a secluded space for her to practice her craft which has fallen into disrepair.

I really liked this concept which was originally created by my team member Martina. Our team consisted of 5 3D artist: me, Martina, Alex, Nate and Giovanni. Alongside this we also had an assigned team mentor to keep us on track Mark Carrier, he was a massive help in providing weekly feedback and helping us navigate how to push the project further. What did I work on?

I was responsible for the modular kit of floors and walls, the technical elements of vertex painting alongside mirror VFX. Due to my challenges on my team I also took on making repetitive modular props such as teh books, papers, paintings and photos around the level. I also did extensive set dressing near the end to add micro narrative around our key focal hero props. Checkout my breakdowns below:

How did development look like?

This was my first team challenge and over the 5 weeks we hit a lot of achievements and hurdles. The first 2 weeks were creating an idea and blockout to follow which proved a challenge since we had a lot of fun ideas to explore. The team also consisted of a lot of different skill sets of modelling, sculpting and texturing so early on it was important to have a concept that everyone could show something off on. Initially we explored an exterior observatory scene taking the concept of from the void as literal void of space. I may honestly do this as a personal project as we had some great ideas but ultimately it proved bland and easy to overscope as it was such a large environment. Mark pointed out that from our original concepts the mirror idea was one they kept coming up so we pivoted back to it and pushed forward.

Some issues did emerge though as the mirror idea was to represent a space before and after ruin in the attic using the mirror as a portal. And I did explore this early on, but the issues were prominent already, the reflection would need to be a real-time or baked Cubemap and the texture was quite obviously flat without some proper technical work which we did not have time to polish. I asked my Limitbreak mentor at the time Jon Murphy on this and he gave me some great resources from fantastic games talk from Psychonauts however I realised it would require a lot of back and forth testing beyond the 5 weeks to make it work seamlessly. So, I made a fantasy portal instead which we kept as it could still be a good focal point with its emissive values and points towards the void. Onwards from week 3 we really locked in and focused on production.

Assets wise we divided our work as best as we could skillset wise however a team member early on dropped out so we had our backup Nate pop in to save the day to catch-up on the needed assets. I was assigned the modular kit of the walls and floor as I have done plenty for games before and wanted to keep pushing these skills. I also had papers assigned just for scattering on the floor to create noise and we were off on production.

What went well?

Like I said before I think despite the challenges we faced the result is filled with a strong atmosphere and mood. Each team member spent a lot of time sculpting and building each asset with care for the narrative. I also really enjoyed sculpting wood which I haven’t done often before so it was a lot of fun to create damaged variants. Marks feedback was also super helpful on breaking down the shapes to make them more interesting.

What could have gone better?

With the nature of full-time work, finding time to work on this was hard to handle as team and we ended up cutting it close to each weekly deadline in regards to meeting milestones. We also lost a team member early on and I had to at the end jump on more assets as some members were taking too long. My original task list was the modular kit, papers and mirror shader but I ended up also doing books, paintings, photo frames, desk, texturing closet and table alongside a bunch of decals. I still feel the scene looks empty, and if everyone did an extra repetitive asset we could have filled it in more, alas it is what it is and sometimes you need to jump in so you can push to submit.

What did I learn?

I learnt more on integrating vertex painting for the walls with a multi layered setup for peeling wallpaper and wood planks. I also really enjoyed sculpting wood, using the morph targets to shred pieces up and down was a good trick I will be using to add more wear to my assets. I was super happy to put my technical art skills to work to create dynamic shaders and masks on top of props for breaking up the values of the floor plus create glass. Marks feedback also helped push the visuals of lighting and colour choices for the walls alongside how to better frame our composition using the wall trim and chair placement. He even showed us a great trick of viewing grayscale values if you turn on the windows accessibility setting for black and white, it forces everything into grayscale on a key binding! Super handy!

Final Verdict

Despite it all I did really enjoy working as part of Team 15, were there grievances? Yes, but that’s just what comes with working with others. Sometimes things do not go to plan, and you have to do what is needed to submit and push your level. Coming together to brainstorm and push ideas every meeting was great fun and I loved meeting other creatives from around the globe! I suggest if you are considering joining a team challenge to join one anyways if you can and see how it goes. Seeing the weekly progress of all the teams going at once was great fun and inspiring competition to keep going each week. There is also a ton of other fantastic submissions that I recommend you look into on Artstation, be sure to check out as they are stunning!
